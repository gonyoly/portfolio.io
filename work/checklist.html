Five decisions before applying a supplier CSV

A supplier file tells you what arrived. It does not tell you what you are allowed to change.

1. Which identifier establishes a match?

Agree a matching key and what happens when it is missing, duplicated or conflicts with another identifier. A similar title is not permission to merge two products.

2. Which fields may change?

List the allowed fields and transformations. If only price updates are approved, a new SKU or rewritten description should not silently replace the current value. An empty permission list should mean no changes.

3. What happens when currencies differ?

A number without its currency is not an interchangeable price. In the synthetic example, incoming USD 20.00 is held back against existing ILS 10.00. No exchange rate is guessed.

4. Which formatting is part of the identifier?

The SKU 000021 is not automatically equivalent to 21. Decide whether leading zeros or spaces matter before an importer changes them. A generic CSV is not automatically spreadsheet-safe or compatible with a shop importer.

5. What belongs in the change record?

Retain old and new values, the enabled rule and references to both source records. Keep unresolved records separate. Agree whether rows may be added or removed; the demonstration permits neither.

This is a checklist of decisions, not a universal production procedure. Actual files, agreed rules and the destination still need testing.
